# Editorial standards

An article should resolve a concrete reader problem with accurate reasoning, examples, and a way to check the result. Word count, keyword repetition, and the appearance of firsthand experience are not substitutes for useful content.

- Separate official documented behavior, source-based analysis, illustrative fixtures, and executed observations. Label examples and limitations near the relevant claim.
- Do not invent personal production incidents, conversations, tool trials, prices paid, timings, sample sizes, success rates, or ownership of an AI-generated experiment. AI-created labs without preserved artifacts cannot support historical measurements.
- For executed results, retain the source revision, versions, inputs, commands, exit status, reports, failures, and scope. Use public or synthetic fixtures and remove secrets. A recreated example is new evidence, not retroactive proof of an earlier claim.
- Verify changing product and framework details with primary documentation. Link the source near the claim. Distinguish a PR author's reported outcome from an independent reproduction.
- Explain why a command or test diagnoses the problem and what it cannot establish. Treat undefined business behavior as a question; do not invent policy in a test.
- Match title, description, image, body, and any FAQ schema. Preserve original publication dates and set a real updated date for substantive changes. Avoid unsupported rankings and exaggerated error stories.
- Preserve established URLs when improving articles. Consolidate genuinely overlapping intent with direct 301s, update internal links, and remove merged URLs from feeds and sitemaps. Low Search Console traffic alone is not a deletion criterion.
- Use the actual content schema and categories. Check the build, generated metadata, internal links, downloadable examples, and local preview. A build is not a technical review of the prose.

The repository's older article skill includes a word-count convention and first-person style examples. Those are not evidence requirements or Google approval thresholds. The truthful-content rules above take precedence over any style convention that would encourage unsupported experience.

Google's [AdSense content guidance](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/81904?hl=en) and [helpful-content questions](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) inform the review. Search guidance and AdSense approval are separate systems. No editorial checklist guarantees acceptance.
